Your practice level

Your answers were a mix of 'sometimes' and 'rarely'. The issue is probably less that environmental problems feel foreign and more that it is not yet clear how to start in everyday life. Choosing the convenient option in a busy day is natural for anyone. So read this result not as a verdict that you fall short, but as a sign that there is a lot of room still to switch on. And you already do a few small things, too.

Your life right now

You may sort your recycling but bring your own bag or cup only when you remember, and let plastic packaging or a disposable cup slide by because it is easy. It is not that you feel nothing about it; many people put it off because they do not know where to begin. What matters is not blaming how things are now but noticing the small things you already do. Starting from there makes the first step much lighter. The smaller you start, the lighter it feels and the easier it is to see through.

Tips for your next step

Pick just one thing. The easiest is to keep a tote bag by the door or in your everyday bag at all times. Once that feels normal, widen the circle step by step: use your own cup at cafes, then choose products with less packaging when you shop. Even once a week counts. Aiming for perfect from the start wears you out fast, so let half be enough, and praise yourself on the days you do it, which is a real secret to sticking with it. If you skip a day, simply pick it back up, and that is enough.

Ideas by area of daily life

While shopping, check whether you have a tote bag before you accept a plastic bag. At cafes, ask for a mug at least when you drink in, and in the kitchen start by storing leftovers in a container. For getting around, try walking a one-stop distance, and online, turn off notifications from apps and emails you never use. Choose things that cost nothing to begin with. Even one of them done is already a good enough step.
