All 8 crying types

Crying profile

The Brave-Faced Wedding Guest lands on Santa’s good side on all three axes: holding back, happy tears and crying in plain sight. Their tear button gets pressed mostly by other people’s happy moments, and even then the first move is to clench the jaw and hang on. They never hide, though. When a tear finally escapes, out comes the handkerchief, pressed to the eye with dignity. Friends often use them as a gauge: if this person is crying, the moment must be truly special. Misty eyes at a celebration are proof of someone who is truly happy for others.

Behind the verdict

Rule 1 of the Santa rulebook is simple: you better not cry. This type holds it together through most moments, so one point. Rule 2 exempts tears of joy, and nearly all of theirs are shed at celebrations, so a second point. Rule 3 treats crying in secret as concealment and crying in front of others as turning yourself in, and they never hide, so a third point. A perfect score means the gift is approved without debate. This is the file the reviewers stamp fastest.

Tips for the next review

The next review should go just as smoothly, but always holding back can leave little room to check in with your own feelings. At weddings, graduations and other moments made for crying, let your jaw relax a little. Santa’s official position is that happy tears are a waste to hold in. Keep a small handkerchief in your bag, and you will have the option to dab instead of hold.

Best crying buddies

Sit next to The Happy Waterworks and the roles divide perfectly: they cry first and loudest, and you hand over tissues while letting one tear slip on cue. For The Undercover Shower Sobber you are a quiet role model. Watching you cry calmly instead of hiding helps them believe it might be fine to cry in front of people, too.

Crying profile

The Secret End-Credits Sniffler holds it in well, melts at moving stories and would rather not be seen crying. Their tear button tends to get pressed when they are lost in something alone, such as a film, a book or an old song, and even then the tears come without a sound. Their timing is near professional: they know exactly how long until the lights come up and have their eyes dry by then. They may look calm on the outside, but their store of moving moments is always full.

Behind the verdict

Under Rule 1 (holding back) they sail through most scenes without a sound, so one point. Under Rule 2 (the joy exemption) nearly all their tears come from being moved, so a second point. Rule 3 is where it slipped: Rudolph’s nose caught the damp sleeve, and that counted as concealment. Two points still means the gift is approved, though rumor has it the reviewers said a little less hiding would have earned a perfect score.

Tips for the next review

If you are going for a perfect score next time, share a moving story instead of keeping it to yourself. A simple “that scene got me” counts as turning yourself in under Rule 3. At the movies, bring a handkerchief instead of using your sleeve, and you will leave less evidence. Sharing what moved you also makes it easier for others to open up, and conversations can get deeper than you expect. Keeping a list of your favorite movie moments makes them easy to pull out when you want to share, and comparing it with a friend’s list is fun too.

Best crying buddies

With The 2 A.M. Pillow Soaker you share the same soft spots, so swapping favorite films is a natural fit. You both cry in private, so trading reviews over text may feel easiest. The Live-Streamed Meltdown is your opposite, which is exactly why it works. They say everything out loud on the spot, and that gives you the nerve to share your own feelings too.

Crying profile

The Lip-Biting Meeting Survivor hangs on even when it hurts, and stays put instead of slipping away. Hurtful comments often get a quick “I’m fine,” and even if the voice wobbles, they do not leave the room. People around them see someone strong and dependable, but quite often they are biting their lip and pressing it all down. Their sense of responsibility runs deep, so the situation gets handled before the feelings do. That steadiness lets the people around them hold steady too, but it also makes this a type that tends to put its own feelings last.

Behind the verdict

The Rule 2 exemption only covers tears of joy, so tears of hurt get no reduction. Under Rule 1, though, the effort to hold it in all the way earned one point, and under Rule 3, standing their ground in the room instead of hiding counted as turning themselves in, for a second point. Two points means the gift is approved. The case file even carries a note: “Held on longer than they cried.”

Tips for the next review

You will pass the next review too, but save some of the energy you spend holding back. Simply telling a trusted coworker or friend after the meeting, “honestly, that stung,” can lighten the load. Holding a warm mug helps as well. Gripping a cup instead of biting your lip softens your face and buys you a moment to breathe.

Best crying buddies

The Live-Streamed Meltdown makes a great ally. They say on the spot the very words you could not bring yourself to say. With The Secret End-Credits Sniffler you trade quiet comfort. You both handle feelings without much noise, so handing each other a coffee without a word is enough to say “I see your day.”

Crying profile

The Undercover Shower Sobber holds it together all day, then lets it all out the moment they are alone. When something hurts, their face does not change on the spot. Then they get home, turn on the shower and hand everything over to the sound of running water. They care deeply about not worrying others, and they have the strength to sort out their own feelings. The defining trait, though, is that nobody ever knows it happened. When the water stops, they step out looking refreshed and finish the day as usual, and that quick switch is this type’s hidden talent.

Behind the verdict

Rule 1 awarded a point for getting through the whole day without cracking. But the tears came from hurt, so no Rule 2 exemption, and under Rule 3 the attempt to drown them out with the shower was ruled concealment. The steamed-up mirror was the decisive evidence. One point means the gift is under review. Under review is not a rejection: it means the reviewers are waiting for an additional statement.

Tips for the next review

The fastest way to turn “under review” into “approved” is an additional statement, which is to say, opening up. Even one text to someone close, like “things have been rough lately,” counts as turning yourself in under Rule 3. Crying alone is not a bad thing, but carrying everything by yourself every time wears you out. After the shower, give yourself one more pat on the back with a favorite song or a warm drink. Now and then, letting your feelings out on a walk instead of in the shower works too.

Best crying buddies

The Under-the-Covers Wailer is a fellow hider, so a lot goes unspoken between you. If you become the kind of friends who ask each other “were you okay today?”, both your scores will go up. The Brave-Faced Wedding Guest is a good role model. Watching someone hold back when needed but cry calmly in front of people shows that feelings do not have to be hidden.

Crying profile

The Happy Waterworks cries fast, cries mostly from being moved, and never hides it. A dog reunion video, the moment someone passes an audition, a friend’s heartfelt confession: whenever something touches their heart, the taps open, wherever they happen to be. Their empathy runs high, so they feel other people’s joy as their own and express it honestly. As a result, people tend to open up easily around them.

Behind the verdict

On tears logged, this type is far ahead of everyone else under review, so no point under Rule 1 (holding back). But every tear on record was confirmed as a tear of joy or of being moved, which earns a point under the Rule 2 exemption, and not one was hidden, which earns a point for turning themselves in under Rule 3. Two points means the gift is approved. The record also notes a short recess, called when Investigator Rudolph started crying at the evidence video.

Tips for the next review

The next review is nothing to worry about, but you might stock up on tissues. If you are giving a speech or a toast, rehearse it out loud once beforehand. Knowing which line will hit hardest lets you take a breath right before it. Being easily moved is a real strength, so do not be embarrassed by the tears. Take them as a sign of how much something meant to you. Stash tissues and a handkerchief in two different pockets of your bag, and you are ready to turn off the taps anywhere.

Best crying buddies

Pair up with The Brave-Faced Wedding Guest and you are the ultimate wedding duo: you cry first and break the ice, and they make sure there is a handkerchief within reach. For The Under-the-Covers Wailer you can be a friendly emergency exit. Seeing you cry openly gives them the courage to come out from under the blanket and cry together.

Crying profile

The 2 A.M. Pillow Soaker is easily moved and quick to tear up, but only ever lets it show when completely alone. Late at night, when the day is done, a song they happen to hear again, an old photo or one short video can bring everything rushing in at once. By day they come across as cheerful and easygoing, yet they are full of delicate feelings. Hugging the pillow so nobody finds out is part of the routine. Morning can make last night’s feelings seem a little embarrassing, but feeling the world that finely is exactly this type’s greatest charm.

Behind the verdict

Under Rule 2, tears of being moved earn the exemption, so one point. But the feelings burst out quickly, so no point under Rule 1, and the record of quiet crying under the covers at night counts as concealment under Rule 3. The pillow humidity Rudolph measured at 2 A.M. was the decisive evidence. One point means the gift is under review. Adding a statement in which you share what moved you would allow a second look.

Tips for the next review

The key to a second look is the morning share. Send a friend the song or video that got you last night, with a line like “this made me cry.” Shared feelings count as turning yourself in under Rule 3. Late-night feelings can eat into your sleep, so try putting your phone a little farther away before bed. A spare pillowcase would not hurt either.

Best crying buddies

The Secret End-Credits Sniffler shares your soft spots, so swapping late-night playlists works well. You both cry in private, so trading reactions over text feels easy. The Lip-Biting Meeting Survivor balances you out: their steadiness holds your wobbly nights in place, and your sensitivity notices the feelings they let slip by.

Crying profile

The Live-Streamed Meltdown tears up the instant something feels unfair and shows it all, right there, with nothing hidden. A late package, being left out of the group chat, an unfair remark: the feeling shows up on their face and in their voice in real time. They are refreshingly honest and do not hold grudges long, so after one good cry they are back on their feet. People often say they are easy to be around because you always know where you stand.

Behind the verdict

The tears burst out right away, so no point under Rule 1, and since they were tears of hurt, no Rule 2 exemption either. But every cry was broadcast live on the spot, which counts as turning yourself in under Rule 3, for one point. It was so transparent that Investigator Rudolph wrote “no investigation needed.” One point means the gift is under review, though this file lists more mitigating circumstances than any other.

Tips for the next review

To turn “under review” into “approved,” practice taking three breaths when the hurt rises. The goal is not to hold the feelings in, but to put them into words before they burst out. Just saying “that hurt a little” can shrink the flood. Honesty is your greatest strength, and when you use it in words instead of tears, people understand you far better. Jotting down what hurt at the end of the day also helps the same thing shake you far less the next time.

Best crying buddies

The Lip-Biting Meeting Survivor has what you need and needs what you have. You speak up about the unfairness on their behalf, and they teach you how to take a breath first. With The 2 A.M. Pillow Soaker your emotional temperatures match. Feelings they usually swallow alone come out easily in front of you, and together the late nights feel less lonely.

Crying profile

The Under-the-Covers Wailer is hit hard by hurt, quick to tear up, and determined to keep it all hidden. The day’s little disappointments pile up and come crashing in at night, and under the covers they go, crying their heart out. The harder they work to keep a straight face in public, the bigger the release in their own space. And after a good cry, they have the resilience to get up the next morning as if nothing happened. That resilience shows they know how to pull themselves together alone, so despite appearances, they are actually quite strong.

Behind the verdict

Under Rule 1 the tears burst out right away, under Rule 2 they were tears of hurt with no exemption, and under Rule 3 the cover-up under the blanket was caught. Zero points across all three rules, so, sadly, the verdict is coal in the stocking. That said, every reviewer added a note saying they were worried about you, verdict aside, and Santa quietly tucked a handkerchief in with the coal. The verdict may be cold, but the ledger is warm.

Tips for the next review

The easiest way to overturn the verdict next time is to take one step out from under the blanket. On a night when you feel like crying, message one person you trust with “today kind of hurt,” and Rule 3 lightens your sentence on the spot. Instead of letting disappointments pile up until night, jot them down as they happen, however briefly. And on nights when the tears still come, keep a glass of warm water and plenty of tissues close.

Best crying buddies

The Undercover Shower Sobber is a fellow hider. Since you know each other’s secret spots, it helps to become friends who casually check in with “was the blanket shaking tonight?” The Happy Waterworks is your reliable emergency exit. Next to someone who cries without a second thought, you can pull off the blanket and cry together. Crying together cuts the hurt in half.
