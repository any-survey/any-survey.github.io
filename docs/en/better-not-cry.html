North Pole Bureau of Tears

Better Not Cry! Will Santa Bring You a Gift This Year?

Investigator Rudolph’s tear record review

What is the Better Not Cry test?

You better not cry, the old warning goes, because Santa skips the kids who do. The North Pole has decided to take that warning seriously, and starting this Christmas it reviews the tear records of grown-ups, too. This Christmas quiz looks at how well you hold back tears, what kind of moments set them off and where you go when they come, then delivers a tongue-in-cheek verdict on whether Santa will bring you a gift this year.

Your investigator: Rudolph

The case is handled by Investigator Rudolph, the reindeer with the red nose. The nose that once lit the sleigh’s way on a foggy night is now trained on the scent of tears. Rudolph goes through a full year of records one page at a time, checking when each incident happened and the evidence photo left at the scene, then takes down the subject’s statement. A morning alarm, a package that showed up late, the final scene of a movie watched alone: in Rudolph’s case file, even the smallest moment counts as an incident.

Grown-ups have plenty to cry about

As kids, we cried over scraped knees and toys that got taken away. As grown-ups, the reasons change. We cry because a deadline wore us down, because an old song caught us off guard, because someone said something kind and meant it. Tears are a natural sign that something moved us, and crying is nothing to be ashamed of. This review is not about scolding anyone for crying. It is a playful way to look back on a year of feelings you worked hard to carry. (This test is just for fun.)

Three things Rudolph looks at

The review looks at three things. First, whether you hold tears back or let them burst out right away. Second, whether your tear button is pressed by joy and being moved, or by hurt and unfairness. Third, whether you cry in front of people or alone where nobody can see. The Santa rulebook has a clause for each of these, and which ones work in your favor only becomes clear once the review is over.

How the review works

Enter your name for the case file and start, and nine tear records come out of Rudolph’s file one page at a time. Look at the evidence photo for each record and pick the statement closest to how you reacted. It takes about a minute. When you turn the last page, an official notice with your name on it is issued, and you can share it with friends to see who is getting a gift. The verdict stays sealed, like an unopened notice, until the very end!
