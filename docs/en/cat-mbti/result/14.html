Personality traits

Introversion (I), Sensing (S), Thinking (T) and Judging (J) make the ISTJ cat a stickler for principle. It concentrates well and applies past experience calmly. What it sets out to do, it finishes; disliking attention, it keeps its own place quietly. Not flashy, but its days never drift out of order — the most dependable of the sixteen.

Everyday behavior

It follows a regular circuit, moving to the same place at the same time each day, and once a hunting game starts it sticks with it until it wins. New food or new litter is not accepted immediately — it observes for a few days before switching. In a commotion it does not join in but withdraws to its own spot and watches quietly.

Care tips

The key is never to change several things at once. When switching food, mix it in gradually over about a week. Keep feeding times regular and the sleeping place fixed, and its stress stays close to zero. Because it is so quiet, warning signs are easy to miss — if it deviates from its usual circuit, check on it early.

Best matches

With the attentive ESFJ cat their rhythms align and both end up comfortable. The matter-of-fact ISTP cat coexists with it, each spending quiet time separately, and the order-loving ESTJ cat shares its sense of rules, so clashes are few. Around impulsive Perceiving (P) types, however, the shape of the day keeps falling out of sync.
