Personality traits

Extraversion (E), Sensing (S), Thinking (T) and Judging (J) make the ESTJ cat the self-appointed head of the household. Once a rule or an order of things is set, it holds everyone to it, and when it has an objective it drives at it without glancing sideways. Practical and decisive, it is never in two minds — which also means its own way is fixed and compromise takes time.

Everyday behavior

It appears at the right spot exactly at mealtime and waits, and it protests unmistakably when another animal uses its bowl or its place. This is the type that comes to wake you when you sleep in. Faced with the vacuum cleaner or an unfamiliar noise it walks over to check rather than fleeing — less fearlessness than a need to keep the situation under control.

Care tips

A predictable day is this cat’s greatest reassurance. Do not casually change the time or place of meals, play and sleep; if you must, move them over several days. In a multi-cat home, provide generous numbers of bowls and litter trays so there is nothing to fight over. Reward it the moment it follows a rule and it learns fast.

Best matches

With the devoted ISFJ cat, leader and follower sort themselves out naturally and friction stays low. The rule-minded ISTJ cat shares its principles, so the two coexist quietly, and with the fellow leader ENTJ cat a clear division of territory turns rivalry into mutual recognition. Free-wheeling Perceiving (P) types are where the order-of-things arguments start.
