Personality traits

The ENTJ dog combines Extraversion (E), Intuition (N), Thinking (T) and Judging (J) into a born commander. In a new situation it does not hesitate: it decides where things are headed and walks out in front. Goals outrank feelings, so anything it starts has to be finished, and at home it keeps a favorite spot with a view of everyone, much like a command post. It approaches other dogs head-on and tends to set the rules of the game.

Walks & play

On a walk, the ENTJ dog is the one choosing the route. At a fork it leans on the leash toward the way it wants, and once it likes a path it picks up speed and heads straight on. Sniffing breaks are short because arriving matters more, and at the park it makes a quick lap of the whole area first, as if on inspection. In fetch or tug it wants to win, and when other dogs gather it slides naturally into the role of play leader.

Living together

This dog settles when it has a job, so teach cues like sit and wait as step-by-step tasks and it will follow eagerly. Pointless repetition bores it, so keep sessions short with a clear goal. Meet leash pulling with a consistent stop-and-wait instead of being dragged along. Mixed rules among family members confuse it, so agree on one set, and leave a puzzle toy for the hours you are out so it can spend its energy.

Best buddies

The ISFJ dog quietly keeps pace beside a front-runner, so walking speeds match. The tender INFP dog tends to find the ENTJ’s lead reassuring, and the ENFJ dog, also Extraverted and Judging, looks after a group in a similar way, so the two click. Meeting another commander type, though, can spark a standoff over a single toy, so introduce them at a distance first.

Personality traits

The ENFP dog blends Extraversion (E), Intuition (N), Feeling (F) and Perceiving (P) into a free spirit whose curiosity spreads in every direction. A stranger or an odd noise has to be checked up close, and its attention hops to whatever just appeared rather than clinging to one thing. Its mood shows on its face and tail, so delight and disappointment are both impossible to hide. A day full of surprises thrills it more than one that runs to plan.

Walks & play

On walks, everything from the base of a tree to a bush to a passing bicycle is an expedition, so the pace stops often and the direction changes without warning. A repeated route fades quickly, yet changing just one street makes its eyes light up. It prefers free-for-all romping and rolling to games with rules, and it will greet a dog it has just met with a play bow right away.

Living together

Attention spans run short, so train in one- or two-minute bursts, mixing treats and praise so it feels like play. For the jumping and excitement at the front door, wait until it calms down and reward that, and the habit eases gradually. Changing the walking route now and then and rotating new toys cuts down the mischief that boredom brings. Because it rushes toward anything unfamiliar, hold the leash firmly and keep the front door and yard gate secure.

Best buddies

The ISTJ dog steadies a bouncy ENFP with its calm routine, so the balance works. The perceptive INFJ dog absorbs the ENFP’s feelings well, and the ESFP dog, also Extraverted and Feeling, matches its play energy for good romping together. A friend who polices every rule too strictly, however, plays so differently that both may end up frustrated, so keep first meetings short.

Personality traits

The ENTP dog is a trickster built from Extraversion (E), Intuition (N), Thinking (T) and Perceiving (P). It works out by playing what makes a treat appear or a door swing open, then puts that knowledge to use at once. It has to test every rule at least once, so something you said no to may quietly be tried again. It is curiosity rather than spite, and a funny reaction from you only encourages the mischief.

Walks & play

Even on a walk it checks the places where something might be hiding, such as the foot of a wall or an unfamiliar alley, before the plain path. When the leash goes slack it sneaks a turn, and it will start a playful chase with any passing dog. In play it prefers hide-and-seek, treasure hunts and tag with made-up rules over simple fetch repetition, and the same toy only holds its interest when the way it is thrown keeps changing.

Living together

Being clever, it gets bored fast, and when bored it goes shopping for entertainment in the trash can or your shoes. Add brain work once or twice a day, such as a sniffing mat, a food puzzle or teaching a simple trick. Training suits adding one new cue at a time better than repeating a fixed routine. Giving in to demands only breeds more of them, so avoid consistently rewarding the barking or pawing that has won it treats before.

Best buddies

The INFJ dog quietly tolerates the ENTP’s pranks yet signals when a line is crossed, making a good counterweight. The hands-on ISTP dog enjoys challenge games like fishing a treat out of a toy, and the high-energy ENFP dog matches it in endless chases. A friend who values fixed routines, by contrast, does not get the jokes, and both can end up worn out.

Personality traits

The ENFJ dog balances Extraversion (E), Intuition (N), Feeling (F) and Judging (J) into a warm guide. It quickly notices which family member is low and which dog is hanging back, and goes over to stay close. It relaxes only when everyone is together, and when guests arrive it moves between family and visitor at the door to smooth the mood. Praise means the world to it, so it works hard to live up to what you expect.

Walks & play

On walks it matches your stride rather than setting its own pace, and glances back to wait if someone lags or tires. At the park it tends to gather the play group together and sometimes acts as go-between, bringing a shy dog into the game. It likes give-and-take play with you and group games with several friends more than solo play, and it responds even more eagerly when people laugh.

Living together

Attention is its fuel, so long stretches alone can leave it glum or show up as behavior that resembles separation anxiety. Keep departures and greetings calm, and welcome it warmly when you return. Praise and eye contact make powerful training rewards. A raised voice, on the other hand, shrinks it more than necessary, so use a low, firm tone. It can wear itself out caring for everyone, so give it a quiet resting place of its own.

Best buddies

The soft-hearted INFP dog opens up with relief thanks to the ENFJ’s attentive care. The ESFJ dog, another family-minded type, looks after routine and its person in a similar way, so the household feels warm when they are together. The quietly loyal ISFP dog also tends to relax in the ENFJ’s kindness. Crowding a friend who values a personal territory, however, can feel like pressure, so watch the distance.

Personality traits

The ESFP dog mixes Extraversion (E), Sensing (S), Feeling (F) and Perceiving (P) into a mood maker who enjoys this very moment with its whole body. It reacts to the smell, sound and touch in front of it rather than to imagined plans, and the more people around, the brighter it shines. Given attention it wags hard and performs tricks, and if the praise pauses it cheerfully goes looking for fun elsewhere.

Walks & play

On the trail it greets every passing person and dog, which slows the walk to a crawl. It would rather receive a pat than sniff the ground, and may march straight toward someone likely to pet it. The best play is the physical kind, so water splashing, ball chasing and rolling in fallen leaves all send it into improvised joy. If someone is watching, it leaps even bigger than usual.

Living together

Because it loves people so much, it often jumps on visitors or dashes toward strangers on walks. Teach early that sitting and waiting earns the right to say hello. Praise and touch are the best rewards, so training works better the more cheerful your voice. It struggles with being left alone, so play well before you go out, and greet it only after the excitement has settled when you return, which makes the house much calmer.

Best buddies

The quietly loyal ISFJ dog gives the excitable ESFP a sense of calm when it needs to rest. The curious ENFP dog shares its play energy, making the liveliest duo at the park, and the fearless ESTP dog has a similar taste for chases and rough-and-tumble, so they fall into rhythm easily. A friend who wants to be left alone, though, may find the ESFP too friendly, so let them meet with a little distance.

Personality traits

The ESFJ dog blends Extraversion (E), Sensing (S), Feeling (F) and Judging (J) into a family-centered homebody. It relaxes only when the whole family is in one place, and it is first to notice and welcome anyone leaving or returning. Familiar routines and well-worn belongings comfort it more than change does, and when it sees you pleased by something it does, it keeps repeating it, like a diligent keeper of the household.

Walks & play

It likes walks best at the same hour along familiar streets, with a set time to greet the neighbor dogs. It remembers the people and dogs on each stretch of the route and starts wagging before they even meet. In play it prefers the familiar ball game or towel tug with you over new challenges, and when the whole family joins in its energy doubles. It also likes to carry things over and wait for praise.

Living together

Sticking to fixed mealtimes and walk times steadies it, and on days when the routine shifts a lot it may follow you around more than usual. Training improves greatly when everyone in the family uses the same cues and rules. Since it craves praise, mark good moments right away. Indifference hits it hard, so even on busy days keep a moment of eye contact and petting. When you are out, leave a blanket that smells of the family.

Best buddies

The ENFJ dog, another Feeling type, cares for the family in a similar way and the two understand each other well. The quietly loyal ISFJ dog shares its habit of keeping routines, so even under one roof they rarely clash, and the lively ESFP dog romps happily while the ESFJ keeps an eye on things, which balances out. A friend who brings unfamiliar settings or sudden changes may feel strange at first, so let them meet slowly.

Personality traits

The ESTP dog is a man-of-action adventurer made from Extraversion (E), Sensing (S), Thinking (T) and Perceiving (P). The body moves before the thought, so it dives in at puddles, stairs and unfamiliar dogs alike. It reacts at once to anything moving in front of it and cannot ignore a rolling ball or a flying bird. It shakes off mistakes quickly, and it focuses best in games where there is something to win.

Walks & play

A walk is closer to a run, and it surges ahead until the leash goes taut. It spends its energy chasing visible movement rather than slowly reading scents. Besides fetch it loves frisbee, water play and hopping obstacles, and signs of tiredness come late. It often launches into rough, body-first play with other dogs without hesitating, so keep an eye on how intense things get.

Living together

With so much energy, too little exercise gets released as chewed furniture or laps around the house. Give it a real outlet once or twice a day with running or ball play. Training works best as short, action-filled games rather than long sit-down sessions. It tends to forget the come-back signal when excited, so let it loose only in fenced spaces and practice recall steadily with tasty treats.

Best buddies

The quiet but hands-on ISTP dog explores alongside the ESTP’s drive as a dependable partner. The leader-minded ENTJ dog shares its competitive streak, so they push each other in running games, and the sociable ESFP dog likes rough-and-tumble too, making the liveliest pairing at the park. A cautious, timid friend can find a charging greeting threatening, so slow things down for them.

Personality traits

The ESTJ dog is a principled guardian formed from Extraversion (E), Sensing (S), Thinking (T) and Judging (J). It likes mealtimes, walks and bedtime to be fixed, and when the schedule slips it whines or paces to let you know. It judges by what it can see and knows, so it checks unfamiliar visitors and new objects carefully first. It feels responsible for the order of the home and is dependable for it.

Walks & play

It likes to walk the same course in the same order, and if the path is blocked or a detour is needed it may stop and make its objection known. Its stride is steady and strong, and it is efficient, finishing toileting and territory checks at set points. It prefers play with clear rules, so structured games like fetch or sit-and-wait before the throw suit it well. If another dog breaks the order it may bark firmly to draw a line.

Living together

Consistency is the best gift. Keep meals and walks to a schedule as far as you can, and when the whole family uses the same rules, problem behavior drops noticeably. It picks up training faster the more clearly you show the goal and the order of steps, and a reward right after it succeeds helps. Changing rules or frequently rearranging furniture can make it edgy. Teaching it to sit at a set spot when guests come reduces barking and eases everyone.

Best buddies

The ISTJ dog, another routine lover, has a similar daily rhythm and sense of rules, so the two never cross each other’s lines. The driven ENTJ dog shares its goal focus and makes a good walking partner, and the devoted ISFJ dog respects the ESTJ’s principles and follows quietly along. A free-wheeling friend who acts on impulse breaks the rhythm and can wear it out, so start with short meetings.

Personality traits

The INFP dog fuses Introversion (I), Intuition (N), Feeling (F) and Perceiving (P) into a tender soul. Loud noises or unfamiliar places make it step back and take stock, yet once it opens its heart it is endlessly sweet. It reads your mood from your face and voice and, on a low day, sits beside you without a sound. It often gazes out the window as if lost in thought or burrows into a favorite blanket.

Walks & play

Walks suit it best at quiet times of day on quiet routes, with few people and dogs around. Training works better when it understands the principle than through drilling, so break things into small steps and show them slowly. It can drift into thought and miss a cue on walks, so practicing name recall and an attention signal at ordinary moments helps.

Best buddies
