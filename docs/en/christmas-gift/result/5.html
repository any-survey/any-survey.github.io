Gift style profile

The Surprise Santa is where heartfelt, planned and experience meet. A gift is all about the feeling at the moment of receiving, and to create that moment the preparation is as thorough as a screenplay. Who to invite, when to turn off the lights and which song to play are the details that make or break the emotion. The time spent imagining the receiver being startled and moved is itself part of the fun for this director at heart.

What you tend to give

The picks are experiences like a surprise party, a video letter from friends, a secret trip to a place full of memories, or an evening built around songs the receiver loves. The receiver’s schedule is quietly arranged, roles are handed out to friends who become accomplices, and a checklist and timeline are drawn up personally. To avoid leaking a clue before the big moment, the cover story is performed quite skillfully. On the day, photos or video are taken so the emotion lives on as a record.

Gift-picking tips

The more secrets, the more variables, so prepare one backup plan for when things go sideways. Some people find surprise events stressful, so check beforehand whether the receiver enjoys crowds and being the center of attention. So that the staging never makes you miss the actual reaction, focus on the single most important scene. The hard parts of the preparation make good stories to share later with a laugh. To leave sincerity behind rather than just shock, finish by saying a short line yourself.

Giving and receiving matches

The best matches for giving and receiving are the Romantic Santa and the All-Booked Santa. The Romantic Santa breathes life into the script with a spontaneous sense of atmosphere, keeping the staging from feeling stiff. The All-Booked Santa is a fellow planner who loves experiences and handles venues, reservations and routes, so full attention can go to the emotional staging. When a prepared script meets an improvised scene, the day becomes a long-lasting memory.
