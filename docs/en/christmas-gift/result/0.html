Gift style profile

The Wishlist Sniper Santa sits at one end of all three axes: practical, planned and object. A gift is worth what the receiver actually uses and enjoys, and to be sure of that no amount of advance research feels like too much. Hints dropped in everyday conversation are remembered, sorted into a shortlist, and turned into a concrete object that carries the feeling. The biggest reward is hearing "this is exactly what I wanted" right after the gift changes hands.

What you tend to give

The picks tend to be something the receiver mentioned in passing, or a replacement for something that has started to look worn. The shortlist gets narrowed to two or three, reviews and ways of use are compared, and risky details such as size and color are checked ahead of time. Wrapping stays neat but the effort goes into the usefulness of the gift itself, and how easy an exchange would be is quietly noted too. As a result, a gift from this Santa rarely ends up forgotten in a drawer.

Gift-picking tips

An accurate gift benefits from one extra signal that it was chosen with feeling. Adding a single sentence when handing it over, such as "I remembered you said you wanted this", changes how warm it feels to the receiver. Now and then, add a small extra that was not on the wishlist. Instead of researching everything, leaving room for one adventurous pick outside the usual taste also adds fun. The longer the preparation, the bigger the anticipation on the day, so give the wrapping and the way of handing over some thought too.

Giving and receiving matches

The best matches for giving and receiving are the Handmade Santa and the All-Booked Santa. The Handmade Santa shares the planning habit but adds handmade effort, giving a story to an accurate gift. The All-Booked Santa is a planner with a practical streak, so schedules line up easily, and one gives objects while the other gives experiences to widen the range of presents. Both prepare ahead, so there is little worry that the exchange will fizzle out.
