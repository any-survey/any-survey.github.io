All 4 AI mate types

How you relate to AI

The Secretary type is high on both formal tone and delegating. Instead of treating AI like a close companion, this type keeps it as a capable secretary that takes an instruction and returns a result, with a clearly drawn line around the role. Greetings and small talk are trimmed away because the real value lies in saving time and focus, and the test of good AI use is whether a usable result arrives in one go. The relationship may look businesslike, but it is the style of someone who knows exactly what standard to expect.

How you use AI

Work with a clear deliverable is what gets handed over: email and report drafts, meeting summaries, schedule and to-do cleanup, and fitting a document into a required format. Instructions stay short, a sentence or two, but conditions such as length, format and the recipient are never left out. When the result arrives, the first check is whether it is usable as it is, and if something is off, the exact spot to fix is pointed out in the next request. This is the type that leans on AI most to clear the repetitive chores of the day.

Tips for using AI better

Adding one line of context to an instruction cuts rework a lot. Just saying who the text is for, what tone fits and where it will be used changes the texture of the result. When the first try does not fit, point at the exact part to change, such as "make the second paragraph shorter", instead of asking for a rewrite from scratch. Saving frequently used instructions and swapping only the conditions saves even more time. Occasionally asking whether a better approach exists once the result is in is also worthwhile.

Things to watch out for

A result written in clean sentences can easily look correct in substance too. Build the habit of checking numbers, dates, names and quoted material against the original source. When handing over a summary job, it also helps to keep only the necessary parts and mask names and contact details before pasting in internal company documents or other people’s information. And remember that the wider the scope handed over, the more the final judgment and responsibility stay with you.

How you relate to AI

The Sidekick type is casual in tone and leans toward delegating. Tossing a request at AI in a relaxed, informal voice comes from feeling no distance, and the low threshold for asking means things often get handed over to see what happens. Rather than agonizing over a perfect prompt, the style is to throw something out and polish it while looking at the output, so trial and error cycles quickly. It is how someone behaves who feels AI is on the same team, not someone to be formal with.

How you use AI

The jobs handed over are light and miscellaneous: name candidates, phrasing ideas, changing the tone of a text, tidying a list, or a first draft for the part where things are stuck. When the result is off, short requests like "again", "more casual" or "cut it in half" keep refining it, with speed as the priority. Instead of expecting a finished version in one go, the shape that is wanted gets found over about three rounds. Because AI is called on lightly several times a day, the number of conversations tends to be high.

Tips for using AI better

The offhand tone can stay as it is; adding just one of length, audience or where it will be used already reduces the number of rounds. When a result turns out well, noting the wording of the request makes it faster to get the same quality next time. Asking for a short explanation of the reasoning at the end shows how the result was put together, so the parts to fix become easy to see. Big jobs come out steadier when split into about three steps instead of being handed over all at once.

Things to watch out for

In a fast back-and-forth it is easy to skim the result and copy it straight out. Make a habit of reading anything meant for other people once, out loud, before it goes out. Because the chat feels as easy as talking to a friend, personal details such as a name, an address or a bank account number can slip in without a thought. Before typing into the box, ask whether this is something that would be fine for others to see. It also helps to remember that a reply that feels in sync is not the same as a reply that is correct.

How you relate to AI

The Friend type is casual in tone and weighted toward learning. AI is seen less as a tool that spits out answers and more as a conversation partner for widening thoughts, and whenever curiosity strikes, the opening line is as light as asking a friend. Instead of getting one answer and stopping, the flow in which each answer sparks another question is the enjoyable part, and the reward comes from the process of understanding deepening. It is the style of someone whose curiosity is the main engine of AI use.

How you use AI

An unfamiliar concept gets explained through an easy analogy, then follow-up questions keep coming, such as "so why is that?" or "what about the opposite case?". Worries get talked through and sorted out, hobbies get chatted about, and views different from your own are asked for. When an explanation is hard, it is easy to ask for a simpler rewording, and once it clicks, it gets restated in your own words. Time goes into the side of the conversation where knowledge builds up the longer it runs.

Tips for using AI better

Explaining what was learned in your own words and asking whether it is right makes understanding much sturdier. When a conversation has run long, ask for a summary of the key points so far to hold on to the thread. Saying how much you already know before asking a question tunes the level of the explanation to you. For anything that needs an exact source or the original text, it helps to add a separate step of looking it up yourself, apart from the chat. If something turns out to be wrong, ask where it differs and why.

Things to watch out for

Because the conversation is easy and enjoyable, it is easy to forget that an explanation can sound confident and still be wrong. For anything important that was newly learned, check it once more against a book or an official source. To keep a balance, make sure AI does not become the only one who hears your worries, and keep talking with people as well. Talking freely about personal matters can also lead to typing in details such as a real name, a workplace or where you live, so keep the habit of writing only as much as needed.

How you relate to AI

The Mentor type is high on both formal tone and learning. Questions are polished politely, in the spirit of asking for guidance, and laid out in an order that makes clear what is wanted. Attitude and structure come first in learning, so courtesy is kept up even in conversation, and an answer is not swallowed whole but broken into steps and explored in depth. It is how someone behaves who keeps AI close as a steady study partner rather than a quick problem-solver.

How you use AI

A concept gets explained step by step on request, and similar concepts are compared or more examples are asked for. A study order may be drawn up, or questions requested to check understanding. Questions naturally carry the current level and the goal, so the direction of the answer tends to fit well. It is common to keep digging into one topic over several days and to write up what was learned in a notebook.

Tips for using AI better

After hearing an explanation, try summarizing it yourself and asking to have only the mistakes pointed out. Whether something has really sunk in shows up not while reading the explanation but while solving practice problems on your own. Writing the current level, the goal and the available time in the first line of a question brings back a better-fitting answer. Passing important claims through a side-by-side comparison with a textbook or official documentation makes the learning sturdier.

Things to watch out for

When guidance is requested politely, the answer that comes back can start to feel like an authority and get believed without question. Even a smooth explanation may contain mistakes or gaps, so core content needs the habit of being checked against other material. Beware of the illusion that reading alone means understanding: real skill comes only from setting aside time to write or solve things yourself. Digging endlessly into one topic can push the actual doing further away, so setting an upper limit on study time helps too.
